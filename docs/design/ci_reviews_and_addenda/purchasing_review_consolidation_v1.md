## 1. The winner — divergence resolved

**Vote:** 2 (dollar curve: GPT, Gemini) vs 1 (continuity: Opus). **Weighted reading: the vote understates Opus**, because Gemini ratified the seeded winner *and reproduced the structural bug the other two caught* (acceleration pinned to dollars; its own "Failure Mode 2 — the curve flattening" notices the bug, then side-steps it), so its hero analysis is the weakest signal. GPT's correction already concedes half of Opus's point (the dollar half must be allowed to fall).

**Resolution — it's a stack, not a single pick; the three agree on its contents and differ only on ordering, which is an acquisition-vs-retention choice:**

- **Value spine (why you buy it; what the CFO underwrites):** the two-curve **Proof Ledger** — hard dollars, gated and *allowed to fall*.
- **Retention spine (why you keep it; where enterprise value compounds):** **continuity / judgment memory** — "everything in this kitchen turns over; this doesn't." Strengthens with account age; does not require the customer to be sloppy.
- **Governance (why you let it act):** **the Refusal** — auto-approve that revokes its own authority. Sits beneath the hero; "nobody buys a brake."

**The unifier (Opus's key structural insight):** **time-to-competence on a never-seen situation** (new supplier / manager / menu / shock) is *simultaneously* the non-saturating acceleration metric for the value story **and** the proof of continuity. One metric, both jobs — which is why the GPT/Gemini-vs-Opus disagreement dissolves rather than needing a winner.

**Recommendation:** lead the **sale** with the gated Proof Ledger (dollars open the door), architect the **product** to manufacture the continuity moment (the Handoff), and carry both on the one moat metric. **The genuine open decision left for you** is the marketing *hero line* — proof-of-dollars ("we prove what we saved, including the weeks we claim zero") vs continuity ("everything turns over; this doesn't"). That's a positioning call worth A/B-testing on real operators, not one to settle from a doc.

## 2. Settled — adopt (convergence across judges)

1. **Kill dollar-acceleration; split #9 into two curves.** *Proof curve* = realized, incremental, counterfactually-gated dollars — **allowed to fall; say so out loud** ("we recovered less this quarter — that's what working looks like," a line a savings-marketing rival can't ship). *Competence curve* = the moat. **[GPT + Opus strong; Gemini exhibits the bug]**
2. **Pin acceleration to time-to-competence on novel situations**, never coverage / accuracy / days-to-detect (all saturate). New suppliers and managers keep arriving, so the metric never runs out of room. **[Opus + prior refinement; Gemini's saturating metric is the counter-example]**
3. **I1 is mostly a false-discovery artifact** (~7 factors × ~30 suppliers × a few hundred orders ≈ 10–20 obs/supplier; max-selection over ~210 noisy estimates yields "your most-trusted factor is your noisiest" under a null where all factors are equal). **Gate before display:** partial pooling across suppliers (not per-supplier max-selection), out-of-sample confirmation (discovered months 1–6 must hold 7–9; in-sample never displays), a minimum evidence floor, and a selection-adjusted statistic — with an honest empty state as the default ("we tested your supplier assumptions; none is reliably misleading yet"). **[All three converge on gate+empty-state; use Opus's pooling+OOS as the method — Gemini's bare "V≥30" is a blunt threshold, not a selection adjustment.]**
4. **Rename I1.** "Supplier Trust Trap" is a defamation-shaped *product name*. Name the capability *signal reliability* / *invoice-variance profile*; the finding is about a **factor** ("the factor that's been lying to you"), never about a named supplier. **[All three]**
5. **Reframe "$30K when Rosa left" off causation.** De-trend against USDA PPI to separate market inflation from lost judgment (Gemini), frame as observation not cause ("in the 90 days after the March staffing change, 14 decisions diverged from a 2-year pattern; here's what each cost"), and **de-identify the person** — naming an individual as the cause of a five-figure loss carries employment/defamation exposure Part C-2 didn't list (Opus). **[All three, combined]**

7. **The Refusal sits beneath the hero** as governance proof, not as a rival hero. **[All three]**
8. **Chain learning = within one legal entity only; transfer operational patterns/rules, never prices/terms.** "Location 4 learns how location 1 *thinks*, not what location 1 *pays*." **[All three]**
9. **Everything additive — nothing in Part B removed.** **[All three]**

## 3. High-value, single-sourced (Opus) — adopt, with one verification

- **The real "won't" is supplier-side economics.** Marketplace fees, GPO rebates, preferred-vendor books. A layer whose judgment says "shift volume off this supplier" is adverse to that revenue, so incumbents won't ship an honest independent recommender against their own rebate book. Sales weapon, not just a moat: *"Ask your current tool who pays them."* **⚠ UNVERIFIED — flagged by Opus as an assumption. Verify per-competitor (BlueCart / MarketMan / Toast+xtraCHEF / MarginEdge / Ottimate monetization models) before it goes anywhere near copy.**
- **Biggest risk = the data budget, not savings credibility.** A single $1.5M restaurant yields only a few thousand line-item decisions/yr across ~15 suppliers × few-hundred SKUs × 4 seasons — every supplier×item×season cell is thin, and that thinness is the *common cause* under I1's false discovery, the year-2-sold-as-year-1 scenarios, the noisy time-to-competence metric, and the trouble proving acceleration in a sales cycle. **Defusal is a beachhead decision:** multi-unit operators + hotel F&B are the **required proof customer** (only they have the decisions/month to make compounding *true* in a two-quarter pilot); the independent owner is the *spreader*. Also resolves chain-learning: multi-unit under one legal entity is where transfer is most valuable and least legally fraught.

## 6. Consolidated legal ranking (frame, not counsel)

1. **Cross-tenant price/terms benchmarking — HIGHEST (antitrust/information-exchange).** Never surface cross-tenant prices/terms. Benchmark against the customer's own history or public indices (USDA PPI) only. Any cross-tenant aggregate → aged, heavily aggregated, non-price *operational* measures, and to counsel first. **[all three; Gemini ranks this #1 overall risk]**
2. **Supplier disparagement — HIGH.** Report only first-party observations about the customer's own account ("your fill rate from them went 96%→81%"), never the supplier's condition/motive/character. P7 "financial stress" is the worst offender. Ban *trap / padding / stress / fraud* from the product vocabulary. **[all three]**
3. **Chain learning across franchisees — HIGH.** Franchisees are typically separate legal entities / local competitors. Transfer non-price operational patterns only; never price/quote/terms across entities. **[Opus + Gemini]**
4. **Employee-referencing findings — (missing from the prompt; add).** Never attribute cost to a named individual; de-identify to "the staffing change." **[Opus]**
5. **Auto-approval financial controls — MODERATE.** Customer sets the ceiling; the system produces the *evidence* to raise it, the human grants it; expansion is an approved, logged event; separation of duties + immutable log + one-click revoke. A "pre-cleared batch, human releases funds" pattern works. **[all three]**
