# Purchasing Copilot — Product-Definition Additions v1
*Additions to `purchasing_copilot_pd_v1_3.md` (Drive/design). Source: the consolidated GPT+Opus+Gemini review (`purchasing_review_consolidation_v1.md`), read as one judgment, not a vote. **Nothing in the 23-scenario set is removed — every change extends or adds.** Mapped to the PD's real sections. These are change-specs for a docs session to apply; the legal framing is for counsel to certify, not a ruling.*

**The decided view (one paragraph).** The product's hero is **continuity — compounding judgment that doesn't quit** ("everything in this kitchen turns over; this doesn't"). It is *proven* in hard dollars by a **two-curve Proof Ledger** (the value spine — why you buy it), *governed* by **the Refusal** (auto-approve that revokes its own authority), and all three are carried by one moat metric — **time-to-competence on a situation never seen before** (new supplier, new manager), which is simultaneously the non-saturating acceleration number and the proof of continuity. The current hero, **I1 the Supplier Trust Trap, is demoted** to a gated discovery example: it's category-entry (any invoice tool can surface quote variance) and, as specified, mostly a false-discovery artifact. Sell it to **multi-unit / hotel F&B first** (the only data budget where compounding becomes provable in a pilot); independents spread it.

## §B — The two-curve Proof Ledger (upgrade §8.2 Weekly Recovered-Dollar Report)

§8.2 today is a flat money trail ("We found $412… net $1,820"). It carries a **structural bug**: a working system removes the leakage it recovers, so recovered dollars *fall* in a healthy account — making "dollars recovered" unusable as a growth/renewal metric and, if forced upward, an incentive to inflate attribution. Split it:

**B1. Proof Curve — hard dollars, allowed to fall.** Every claimed dollar passes an **incremental-attribution gate** before it's shown: counterfactual/holdout ("would a competent buyer, or your own baseline, have caught this?"), no double-counting across detectors, **realized-not-modeled**. The report reads: *"$412 recovered — $310 you'd have missed (here's each), $102 you'd likely have caught."* Falling is success, and the product should **say so**: *"We recovered less this quarter than last. That's what it looks like when it's working."* (A line a savings-marketing rival structurally won't ship.)

**B2. Competence Curve — the moat, allowed to accelerate.** Pin acceleration to **time-to-competence on situations never seen before** (new supplier/manager/menu/shock) — *not* coverage %, accuracy, or automation share, which saturate and can't carry a second-derivative claim past month 9. *"First new supplier: 9 weeks to read a normal delivery. Latest: 11 days."* This is γ / re-convergence in kitchen language, measurable weekly, non-saturating, and the same metric that proves continuity.

**B3. Attribution hierarchy** (progressively more conservative as the claim gets stronger): Flagged → Avoided → Realized → Incremental → Compounding. When evidence is insufficient, **display $0**, not a modeled number.

**B4. Honest $0 is a feature, not a gap** (ties to §D-null below): a clean week reports *"$0 incremental this week — nothing leaked; coverage 94%; we got faster at three things,"* and the competence curve keeps rising even when the dollar curve is flat.

## §C — I1 rehabilitation (revise §3 I1, §7.2 F11, §11.1 vocabulary)

**C1. Rename.** "Supplier Trust Trap" is a defamation-shaped product name. Rename the capability *signal reliability* / *invoice-variance profile*; the finding is about a **factor** ("the factor that's been lying to you"), never about a named supplier. Add to the §11.1 mandatory mapping: **ban** *trap, padding, stress, fraud* from all buyer-facing text.

**C2. Gate before display.** I1 as specified (~7 factors × ~30 suppliers × a few hundred orders ≈ 10–20 obs/supplier) will surface "your most-trusted factor is your noisiest" for a *clean* buyer under a null where all factors are equal — max-selection over ~210 noisy estimates. Before it displays: **partial pooling** across the tenant's suppliers (not per-supplier max-selection), **out-of-sample confirmation** (discovered in months 1–6 must hold in 7–9; in-sample never displays), a **minimum evidence floor**, and a **selection-adjusted statistic**. Default state = the honest empty state (§E, "Not Yet"). *(This "discovery gate" is itself a "won't" moat — a tool that markets on surprising findings won't suppress its own headlines. Name it as a capability.)*

**C3. I1's stronger successor — Yield-Adjusted Quote Audit.** Net **plate cost** vs gross quote, cross-referencing POS depletion / trim-waste: *"$0.20/lb cheaper, but +14% higher final plate cost."* Harder to copy (needs POS yield data) and not defamation-shaped. Add as an extension of I1, not a replacement.

**C4. Demote** I1/F11 from [HERO] to a gated example under continuity in §3, §4, §7.2.

## §D — Reframes & compliance (revise §2 Marco, §3 P5/P7; add a §12 legal subsection)

**D1. Reframe P5 / Marco's "$28–30K when Rosa left"** off causation. Do not attribute a five-figure loss to a named individual (employment + defamation exposure the PD doesn't currently list). De-trend against USDA PPI to separate market inflation from lost judgment, and frame as observation: *"In the 90 days after the March staffing change, 14 order decisions diverged from a pattern that had held two years — here they are, with what each cost."* De-identify the person to "the staffing change." Strengthen later with replay (§E, Counterfactual).

**D2. Reframe P7 "financial stress"** to first-party observation only: *"your fill rate from them went 96%→81% over 8 weeks."* Never assert the supplier's condition, motive, or character.

**D3. New §12 subsection — Legal exposure, ranked (frame, not counsel):**
1. **Cross-tenant price/terms benchmarking — highest** (information-exchange/antitrust). Never surface cross-tenant prices/terms; benchmark against the customer's own history or public indices only.
2. **Supplier disparagement** — first-party observations only; banned vocabulary (C1).
3. **Chain learning across franchisees** — separate legal entities/competitors; transfer non-price operational patterns only (see §F).
4. **Employee-referencing findings — (missing from the PD; add).** Never attribute cost to a named individual; de-identify.
5. **Auto-approval financial controls** — customer sets the ceiling; the system produces the *evidence* to raise it, the human grants it; expansion is a logged, approved event; separation of duties + immutable log + one-click revoke.

## §F — Go-to-market / beachhead (revise §2 personas, §5, §12-Q4/Q5)

**F1. Multi-unit / hotel F&B is the *required proof customer*, not one of four personas.** A single $1.5M restaurant is too thin per supplier×item×season for the compounding claim to become true inside a sales cycle — that thinness is the common cause under I1's false discovery, the year-2 scenarios, the noisy time-to-competence metric, and the trouble proving acceleration. Only **Lisa (chain) and Rafael (hotel F&B)** have the decisions/month to prove it in a two-quarter pilot. **Marco (independent) is the *spreader*** — he repeats the line; he's the wrong customer to prove it on. This also resolves chain-learning: multi-unit under **one legal entity** is where transfer is most valuable and least legally fraught.

**F2. The real "won't" — supplier-side economics ⚠ UNVERIFIED.** Marketplace fees, GPO rebates, preferred-vendor books mean a layer that says "shift volume off this supplier" is adverse to incumbents' revenue — so they won't ship an honest independent recommender against their own rebate book: *"Ask your current tool who pays them."* This is the sharpest competitive weapon here **but it rests on how BlueCart/MarketMan/Toast+xtraCHEF/MarginEdge/Ottimate actually monetize — verify per-competitor before it goes in any copy.**
